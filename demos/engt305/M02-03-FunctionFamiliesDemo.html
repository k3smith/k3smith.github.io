Key idea: The domain of a function is the set of x values it accepts, and the range is the set of y values it can produce. A function is even if f(−x) = f(x), which makes its graph a mirror image across the y axis, and odd if f(−x) = −f(x), which makes the graph look the same after a half turn about the origin. Most functions are neither. The inverse of f undoes it: if f takes x to y, the inverse takes y back to x. Its graph is the reflection of f across the line y = x, and it is a function only when f is one-to-one, meaning no horizontal line crosses the graph of f more than once.

The families at a glance
Linear\(y = m x + b\)Constant slope
Quadratic\(y = a(x-h)^2 + k\)One turn, at the vertex (h, k)
Power\(y = a x^n + k\)Polynomial; even n is U shaped, odd n is S shaped
Exponential\(y = a e^{k x} + c\)Flat on one side, runaway on the other
Logarithmic\(y = a \ln x + c\)Only x > 0; steep near zero, then slow
Gaussian\(y = A e^{-(x-\mu)^2 / (2\sigma^2)}\)Bell: one peak, flat tails

Linear, quadratic and power functions are all polynomials: sums of whole-number powers of x. The quiz groups quadratic and power together under that name.

In practice: Recognizing the family tells you what to expect outside the range you measured, and which model to fit. Linear: a spring's force against stretch. Quadratic: head loss in a pipe against flow rate. Power: outflow through a basin outlet against water depth (Module 8 and the capstone). Exponential: a tank draining or a part cooling (Module 6). Logarithmic: the scale behind decibels and pH, and the reason log axes straighten power laws. Gaussian: the bell curve of measurement scatter (Module 7). In MATLAB, write a family as an anonymous function, for example f = @(x) a*exp(k*x) + c; and plot it with fplot(f, [-8 8]). When x is a vector, powers and products need the element-wise operators: a*x.^2, not a*x^2.
