Key idea: The annual exceedance probability P is the chance that a given storm size is exceeded in any single year. From a record of n yearly maximums, rank them from largest (m = 1) to smallest (m = n) and estimate P = m/(n + 1), a formula known as the Weibull plotting position. The return period is T = 1/P, the average number of years between exceedances over a very long time. It is an average, not a schedule. If each year is independent, the chance of escaping N years in a row is (1 − P)N, so the risk of at least one exceedance in N years is 1 − (1 − P)N.

$$P = \frac{m}{n+1} \qquad T = \frac{1}{P}$$ $$\text{risk} = 1 - \left(1 - \frac{1}{T}\right)^{N}$$
In practice: In MATLAB: xs = sort(x, 'descend'); m = (1:numel(x))'; P = m/(numel(x)+1); T = 1./P; then risk = 1 - (1 - P).^N. In the capstone (Part G), this is how the design storm for the detention basin gets its return period, and the risk formula says what choosing that storm means: how likely the basin is to be overtopped at least once during its service life. Two limits belong in the discussion. A 30-year record cannot say anything reliable about storms rarer than about once in 30 years; going further means fitting a probability distribution to the data and extrapolating. And the risk formula assumes every year is independent with the same P, which a changing climate or a changing watershed can break.
