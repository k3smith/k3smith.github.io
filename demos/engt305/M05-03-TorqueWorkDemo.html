Torque and Work: Two Jobs for One Force

ENGT 305 · Module 5  |  A force can turn something or move something. The cross product measures the turning, the dot product measures the moving, and each cares about a different part of the force.

Key idea: The same force can do two different jobs, and each is measured by a different vector product. Torque (also called a moment) is the turning effect of a force about a pivot. It is the cross product τ = r × F, where r runs from the pivot to the point the force acts on. Only the part of the force perpendicular to r turns anything, so pushing straight along the lever gives zero torque. Equivalently, torque is the force times its moment arm, the perpendicular distance from the pivot to the force's line of action. Work is the energy a force transfers while its point of application moves through a displacement s. It is the dot product W = F · s. Only the part of the force along the motion does work, so a force perpendicular to the motion does none, and a force opposing it does negative work.

In practice: In MATLAB, torque is cross(r, F) with both written as 3D vectors (use 0 for the z components of a flat problem), and work is dot(F, s). Torque and work share the unit N·m but are different things: torque is a turning effect with a direction, work is an amount of energy, and only work is called a joule. In the capstone (Part E), the water force on the outlet trash rack acts at some distance from its hinge, and the hinge moment is r × F. In 3D the result is a vector, and its component along the hinge line, found with a dot product, is the moment the hinge and latch must resist. A positive z torque here means counterclockwise, following the right-hand rule.
