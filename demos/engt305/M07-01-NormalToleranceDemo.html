Normal Distribution and Tolerance

ENGT 305 · Module 7  |  No process makes every part the same. Set where a process is centered and how much it varies, then see what fraction of its output lands inside the tolerance.

Inside tolerance (yield): Too thin: Too thick:
Key idea: The normal distribution is the bell curve that many measured quantities follow. The mean μ is its center and the standard deviation σ is its spread: about 68% of values fall within 1σ of the mean, 95% within 2σ, and 99.7% within 3σ. The area under the curve between two values is the probability of landing between them. A tolerance is the range a dimension is allowed to fall in, from the lower specification limit (LSL) to the upper specification limit (USL). The area between those limits is the yield, and the area in the tails is the defect rate. Here the example is the thickness of a precast concrete panel with a 150 mm target and a tolerance of plus or minus 6 mm.

$$f(x) = \frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}\, e^{-\frac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}} \qquad z = \frac{x - \mu}{\sigma}$$ $$P(\text{LSL} \le X \le \text{USL}) = \Phi(z_U) - \Phi(z_L)$$

Φ(z) is the standard normal cumulative distribution: the area under the bell curve to the left of z. A z-score counts how many standard deviations a value sits from the mean.

In practice: From a set of measurements x, estimate the two parameters with mean(x) and std(x). The fraction inside the limits is then normcdf(USL, mu, sigma) - normcdf(LSL, mu, sigma) (Statistics and Machine Learning Toolbox), or without the toolbox, Φ(z) = 0.5*erfc(-z/sqrt(2)). The same calculation answers one-sided questions, such as the fraction of concrete cylinders that fall below a required strength. The two ways a process fails look different and call for different fixes: a mean that has drifted is an adjustment problem, while too much spread is a consistency problem that no adjustment will cure.
