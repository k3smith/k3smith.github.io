Key idea: The dot product a · b is a number. It is positive when the angle between the vectors is under 90°, zero when they are perpendicular, and negative past 90°. Divided by |a|, it is the scalar projection: the signed length of b's shadow along a (the purple segment). The cross product a × b is a vector perpendicular to both a and b. Its length equals the area of the parallelogram with sides a and b (shaded green), and its direction follows the right-hand rule: curl the fingers of your right hand from a toward b and your thumb points along a × b.

$$\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b} = a_x b_x + a_y b_y + a_z b_z = |\mathbf{a}|\,|\mathbf{b}|\cos\theta$$ $$\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b} = \big[\,a_y b_z - a_z b_y,\;\; a_z b_x - a_x b_z,\;\; a_x b_y - a_y b_x\,\big]$$ $$|\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}| = |\mathbf{a}|\,|\mathbf{b}|\sin\theta = \text{parallelogram area}$$
In practice: In MATLAB these are dot(a,b), cross(a,b), and norm(v) for a magnitude. The capstone (Part E) uses both products on the outlet trash rack, the grate that keeps debris out of the detention basin outlet. Crossing two edge vectors of a rack panel gives a vector normal (perpendicular) to the panel, and its magnitude is the panel area, which is what a water pressure acts on. Dotting a force with a unit vector gives the component of the force in that direction. The moment of a force F applied at position r from the hinge is the cross product r × F, and dotting that with a unit vector along the hinge axis gives the moment the hinge must resist.
